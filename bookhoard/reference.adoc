= bookhoard reference guide

this guide recordeþ semantic comparisons and design boundaries.

== current surface

`ground.tung` is the ordinary umbrella import. it re-publisheþ the common
data, effect, equality, ordering, algebraic, numeric, collection, and text
surface without becoming an implicit prelude.

the total-order hierarchy addeþ totality to partial order; generic infimal
and supremal semilattices stay independent until a lattice requireþ both.
distributive lattices additionally record boþ distribution laws.
`powerset` is a characteristic function and useþ those generic operations,
whereas `set` and `table` are finite list-backed containers with qualified
constructors.

lawful numeric fills stop at their honest boundary. integers provide exact
euclidean division and the corresponding algebraic frames. floats expose ieee
operations but do not claim exact field or total-order laws.

`bifunctor` captureþ covariant mapping over boþ sides of products and sums.

== lean and mathlib comparison

lean and mathlib are references for algebraic boundaries, lawful naming, and
the separation of finite collection meanings. they are not syntax templates.

mathlib defineþ a
https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Data/Multiset/Defs.html[multiset]
as a list quotiented by permutation, so repetitions remain but order doth not.
it defineþ a
https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Data/Finset/Defs.html[finite set]
as a multiset paired with a proof of no duplicates, and useþ finite sets as a
base for finite sums and products.

tung currently hath neither quotient types nor dependent proof fields. `powerset` is
therefore the characteristic-function alias `a func 𝟚`, matching lean's
predicate view and avoiding an unenforced representation invariant.
its intersection and union are selected through the overloaded infimum and
supremum operations. no bare powerset monoid chooseþ between them: wrap a
powerset in `infimal` for the intersection monoid or `supremal` for the union
monoid.
`set` and `table` expose list-backed `from-list` constructors; operations
built through `empty`, `singleton`, and `put` preserve finite-set uniqueness,
but direct `set.from-list` use may build a noncanonical value. the shared
constructor name is deliberate: `set.from-list` and
`table.from-list` remain distinct through ordinary qualification.

the planned order-insensitive bag needeþ not wait for quotient types. its
representation boundary and implementation work are tracked in
xref:../todo.adoc[`todo.adoc`] rather than described as part of the current api.

mathlib's theorem hierarchy is much richer than tung's checked frame laws.
frame laws in tung are type-checked statements, not proofs and not evaluator
rewrite rules. additions from mathlib should therefore first improve data and
frame boundaries; proof-dependent structures belong after tung gaineþ an
explicit proof story.
